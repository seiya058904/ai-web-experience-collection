# CRAVE — Research and material direction

Research reviewed: **7 October 2026**.

CRAVE examines the visual anticipation of eating through heat, surface, texture,
moisture, contrast, and timing. Food is treated as material, while remaining
recognizably edible. This document separates sourced material facts from authored
visual decisions.

## Selected four-food cast

Three generated Hero directions were compared for appetite, material credibility,
crop, silhouette, color, and motion potential: **A — Laminated**, **B — Seared**,
and **C — Glazed**. Both the main direction review and the independent review
selected **A**. Its dry, brittle crust and soft, layered interior offered the
strongest contrast. B became a supporting heat study. C established the glaze
direction, with an explicit requirement for subtler, less mirror-like gloss.

| Food material | Narrative role | Production criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laminated pastry | Hero / Desire / Crust / Inside | Copper-gold ridges, thin darker tips, irregular flakes, connected pale layers and open pockets. The structure must not resemble perfect cells or stacked cardboard. |
| Seared scallop | Heat / Brown | Golden contact surface, a pearly moist center, and restrained fat sheen at the edge. Reject rubber-like texture, heavy charring, or implausible flesh. |
| Thick chocolate | Melt | A coherent ribbon that folds, accumulates, and slowly relaxes. Highlights follow its curved surface. Reject water-like splashes, black paint, or rope-like stretching. |
| Warm caramel apple tart | Glaze / Steam / Plate / Cut / Finale | A thin amber coating, credible fruit and pastry, a fresh cut face, and sparse steam associated with warmth. Reject glass-like fruit or a cold mirror-glaze appearance. |

These choices are design judgments after visual comparison, not experimental
claims that one food image creates the same appetite response in every viewer.

## Material truth

### Browning is a change in the food surface

Maillard chemistry begins with reactions between reducing sugars and amino
groups. Caramelization concerns sugar chemistry and is distinct from Maillard
browning, although cooked foods can involve both processes. [S1, S2]

**Application:** preserve local variation: golden ridges, darker contact regions,
and lighter recesses. A uniform orange filter is insufficient. Do not assign a
single universal onset temperature to every food, or imply that the same chemical
mechanism explains every brown surface.

### Pastry layers need believable irregularity

King Arthur's documented bake comparison found that fewer, more distinct layers
could produce a more open interior than a larger number of compressed layers in
the tested pastry. Its pastry-temperature guidance explains how steam released
from butter's moisture expands gaps between dough layers. [S3, S4]

**Application:** retain unequal elongated pockets, thin connecting membranes,
curling flake edges, and occasional compressed areas. An open crumb is not a
regular hexagonal lattice. The cut must reveal a connected structure rather than
a field of unrelated holes. More tiny layers are not automatically more credible
or more appetizing.

### Chocolate carries weight

Valrhona describes a well-emulsified ganache as viscous, creamy, smooth, and shiny
with an elastic center. A published experiment on chocolate masses found
non-Newtonian flow behavior, including shear thinning and yield behavior in its
tested samples. The experiment does not define a universal viscosity for ganache
or every chocolate sauce. [S5, S6]

**Application:** give the flow a broad body, a rounded hanging lip, accumulation
where it lands, and slow relaxation. Reflections bend with folds. Avoid watery
sprays and identical motion speeds throughout the liquid.

### Visible steam is not opaque white gas

The Met Office's warm-water experiment explains that cooling warm, moist air
produces tiny visible water droplets. Umeki and colleagues directly studied
microdroplets above hot water and observed their interaction with local airflow.
Their microscopic observations are evidence of droplets, not a literal animation
recipe for steam above pastry. [S7, S8]

**Application:** sparse, translucent strands originate near an exposed warm
surface, drift, and dissipate. Their irregularity and limited opacity are authored
visual choices informed by this mechanism. Steam should not resemble smoke,
symmetrical ribbons, or a fog-machine layer. It should not appear on every food
merely to communicate richness.

### The highlight must reveal the underlying material

Research on material perception identifies highlight coverage, sharpness,
contrast, and spatial structure as cues used in material judgments. Steve
Hansen's own food-lighting demonstration uses directed soft light and a low side
light to reveal dimensional relief and controlled specularity. [S9, S10]

**Application:** keep a coherent dominant light direction, but vary its response:
broken on crust, softer on fat, continuous and bent on chocolate, and thin on
glaze. Preserve detail in brown shadows. A uniform glossy film would erase the
differences between these foods.

## Authored visual decisions

- **Material match cuts:** connect a seared rim to a pastry ridge, a fractured edge
  to an exposed interior, and a curved chocolate highlight to a glaze contour.
  Continuity depends on shape, light, and texture, rather than only crossfading
  between unrelated food images.
- **Rhythm:** hold long enough to read a surface; build tension before fracture or
  cutting; make the break brief; let softness, flow, or steam release the tension.
- **Appetite selection:** reject repeated cells, waxy gloss, artificial color,
  impossible structures, excessive sharpening, and opaque steam. Effects cannot
  repair an unconvincing food asset.
- **The final absence:** a freshly missing piece, exposed interior, a few crumbs,
  and a small sauce trace allow the viewer to imagine the first bite.

These are conceptual, cinematic systems. CRAVE does **not** calculate cooking
chemistry, actual temperatures, viscosity, heat transfer, or a physically validated
steam field. Scroll progression and material language must not be interpreted as
calibrated laboratory measurements or cooking instructions.
