# MAGMA — Research and Material Grounding

Research reviewed: 7 October 2026.

## Scope

**MAGMA is an imagined material study across volcanic states.** Its continuous visual journey compares related materials and compresses geological time. It does not depict the mandatory cooling history of one specimen: basalt and typical obsidian differ in composition as well as cooling history. The GLASS chapter introduces a different cooling history; CRYSTAL · STONE returns to a comparative mineral and stone study. [4, 6]

Scene duration is an editorial choice. Heat, crust, roughness, displacement and transition controls are artistic parameters, not observations, calibrated physical quantities or live telemetry. No numerical temperature, pressure, cooling duration or viscosity should be inferred from the imagery.

## Findings used in the work

- **Surface and interior can diverge.** A crust can form quickly and insulate a much hotter interior. Solidification, the loss of visible glow and cooling to ambient conditions are separate milestones. This supports a darkening surface over a persistent but narrowing internal light. [1]
- **Flow retains material resistance.** Pāhoehoe can have smooth, billowing, ropy surfaces; ʻaʻā is rough and fragmental. Cooling and additional crystals can increase resistance to movement. The deliberately slow motion in MAGMA is not a universal claim about lava speed. [2, 6]
- **Black has several textures.** Field observations include shiny black glassy rinds and waxy grey interiors. These inform selective highlights, mineral grey and differences between exterior and exposed interior. [3]
- **Cracks can advance in events.** Cooling contraction builds stress; a fracture can extend and then pause after stress relief. Authored crack progression expresses this more clearly than constantly redrawing noise. [7]
- **Glass changes how light reveals form.** Typical obsidian is a silica-rich volcanic glass. Its conchoidal fractures can form smooth, curved surfaces and sharp edges. GLASS and FRACTURE use reflections and curved breaks as material observations. [4]
- **Stone geometry and mineral grains are distinct.** Basalt can have a fine-grained groundmass and larger crystals. Columnar joints are fractures through rock, not giant individual crystals; their polygons vary. [5, 8]
- **Pores retain a history of gas.** Vesicles are cavities left by trapped gas during solidification; their shapes can reflect deformation. They should read as irregular features of the material. [2]

These findings guide the imagery. The shaders, transitions, compressed chronology and shared fissure are artistic interpretations rather than a validated thermodynamic simulation.

## Image use and provenance

The cited pages are scientific and visual references. **No photographs from these pages were downloaded or reused by this research; they are not supplied as project assets.** A government-hosted page may include separately credited photographs, so the links should not be treated as a blanket image license.

AI-generated imagery should be identified as an artistic reconstruction, not documentary photography of a real eruption or specimen. Generated key visuals, their selected revisions and any edits belong in the project’s asset provenance record. Procedural masks, geometry and shaders should be identified separately from photographic or generated assets.
