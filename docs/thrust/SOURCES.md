# THRUST — Source Register

The following primary public sources were consulted on **2026-10-06**. This is the access/review date; the sources have different original publication dates.

They inform engineering relationships and visual research. THRUST's engine, blades, aircraft, streamlines and thermal artwork are independently authored. Manufacturer photographs, CAD geometry and diagrams were not used as runtime model assets.

## NASA Glenn: engine relationships

### 1. Turbofan Thrust

NASA Glenn Research Center

https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/turbfan.html

Used for the core/bypass split, the definition of bypass ratio by mass flow, the two-spool concentric-shaft arrangement and the contribution of both streams to thrust.

### 2. Compressors

NASA Glenn Research Center

https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/compress.html

Used for axial-compressor staging and the accumulation of pressure rise through multiple stages.

### 3. Axial Compressors

NASA Glenn Research Center

https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/caxial.html

Used for rotor/stator alternation, the distinction between rotating shaft-mounted blades and stationary casing-mounted blades, and the organization of flow through the compressor.

### 4. Combustor — Burner

NASA Glenn Research Center

https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/burner.html

Used for annular combustion around the central shaft, liner/casing construction, perforations, and the rise in temperature with a slight pressure decrease through combustion.

### 5. Power Turbine

NASA Glenn Research Center

https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/powturb.html

Used for extraction of gas energy into shaft work, separate spool relationships, rotating and stationary blade rows, and compressor-bleed cooling through hollow turbine blades.

### 6. Nozzles

NASA Glenn Research Center

https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/nozzle.html

Used for the relationship between a central core nozzle and an annular fan-flow nozzle, and the return of both flows to the surrounding air.

## NASA: turbine cooling

### 7. Special Projects Laboratory — Turbine Cooling

NASA Glenn Research Center

https://www.nasa.gov/special-projects-laboratory-turbine-cooling/

Used for internal air cooling, impingement and external film cooling. This is a historical research account; historic materials and engine examples are not presented as current product specifications.

### 8. New Concepts in Film Cooling for Turbine Blades

NASA Technology Transfer Program — LEW-TOPS-52

https://technology.nasa.gov/patent/LEW-TOPS-52

Used for the path from compressor bleed into internal blade chambers and out through cooling holes, and the importance of keeping cooling flow near the surface. The illustrative blade does not reproduce the specific patented recess or hole designs described by this page.

## Manufacturer references: form and mechanical layout

### 9. It's Great Design Too: World's Biggest Jet Engine Fan Blade at The Museum of Modern Art

GE Aerospace

https://www.geaerospace.com/news/press-releases/commercial-engines/its-great-design-too-worlds-biggest-jet-engine-fan-blade-museum

Used as historical reference for the visual relationship between swept composite fan-blade surfaces and a protective metallic leading edge. No blade photograph, manufacturer logo, airfoil data or product dimensions were incorporated.

### 10. The GENX (GE Next-Generation) Engine

GE Aerospace

https://www.geaerospace.com/news/tr/press-releases/commercial-engines/genx-ge-next-generation-engine

Used as a public example confirming that the high-pressure compressor and high-pressure turbine share one spool, while the fan, booster and low-pressure turbine share the other. This is an early product-development announcement; its dates, superlatives and preliminary performance figures are not adopted as present-day facts or as THRUST specifications.

## Scope of the research

The sources support the engineering principles summarized in [ENGINEERING.md](ENGINEERING.md). They do not validate the performance of the original concept, the displayed field values, its structural strength or manufacturing feasibility. The project's authored stage counts and illustrative 9:1 bypass ratio are clearly identified as concept choices.

Source names identify their authors. They do not indicate endorsement, certification, sponsorship or participation in THRUST.
