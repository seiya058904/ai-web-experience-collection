# FOSSIL — Research basis / 研究依据

Research date: 7 October 2026.

FOSSIL is an interpretive digital exhibition about how remains become records. The following museum and geological sources informed its material language, scene logic, and terminology. They are factual references, not sources of the generated specimen photography or synthetic scan data.

本文件记录八个权威来源与其在作品中的具体用途。成品里的菊石、琥珀、岩层摄影感图像，以及扫描与局部模型，均为本项目生成或编写的解释性材料；下列资料支持科学叙事，不为虚构标本提供真实馆藏身份。图像和数据的逐项来源见 [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md)。

## 1. Several paths to preservation

**National Park Service — Body Fossils**  
https://www.nps.gov/subjects/fossils/body-fossils.htm

Permineralization fills natural pore spaces with minerals. Replacement substitutes minerals for original material that has dissolved. Fossils may also preserve impressions or other remains, and many are incomplete. The preservation scene therefore presents changes in material and retained shape without suggesting that every organism becomes a complete solid mineral replica.

## 2. A shape can survive its original material

**National Park Service — Molds, Casts, and Steinkerns**  
https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/mold-casts-and-steinkerns.htm

A mold records an impression in surrounding sediment; a cast can form when that space fills with sediment or minerals. An internal shell cast may remain after the shell itself dissolves. Positive form and negative impression are used as related but distinct visual records.

## 3. Sediment becomes a layered record

**U.S. Geological Survey — Fossils, Rocks, and Time: Rocks and Layers**  
https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/fossils/rocks-layers.html

Accumulating sediment can bury remains. Burial and chemical changes can turn sediment into rock; mud may become mudstone or shale. Differences in particle size or composition produce bedding. In an undisturbed succession, lower beds are older. The procedural layers suggest relative sequence and material variation; their thickness is not a measured geological clock.

## 4. Preparation is controlled removal

**American Museum of Natural History — Mechanical Preparation**  
https://www.amnh.org/research/paleontology/vertebrate/fossil-preparation/revealing/techniques/mechanical-preparation

Preparators select tools for the specimen and its matrix, remove material carefully, and support fragile areas. The exposure scene uses small reveal patches, pauses, and retained supporting stone. Its brush gesture represents clearing loose grains; it does not imply that brushing alone removes hard rock.

## 5. Amber preserves through enclosure

**National Park Service — Preserved Remains**  
https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/preserved-remains.htm

Amber is fossil resin and can enclose insects or other organic inclusions. Original material may decay while fine shape remains as a mold. The amber scene emphasizes depth, enclosure, and a preserved trace. It makes no claim of intact living tissue, viable DNA, or biological revival.

## 6. Imaging can reveal the interior

**American Museum of Natural History — Digital Preparation**  
https://www.amnh.org/research/paleontology/vertebrate/fossil-preparation/revealing/techniques/digital-preparation

Micro-CT can reveal internal structure non-destructively and support three-dimensional representations. Results depend on material contrast and geometry; limestone matrix and flat slabs can be difficult. CT and surface scanning are distinct techniques. FOSSIL uses an explicitly interpretive volume to make section depth and reconstruction readable; it is not an acquired scientific scan.

## 7. A chambered shell as a recurring form

**Natural History Museum, London — What is an ammonite?**  
https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/what-is-an-ammonite.html

Ammonites were marine cephalopods with chambered shells. Many shells form a flat spiral, and internal partitions are called septa. Soft-part evidence is much rarer than shell evidence. The exhibition uses a ribbed planispiral shell as a recurring study form and leaves the living animal unreconstructed. External ribs, internal partitions, and drawn contours are not treated as interchangeable anatomy.

## 8. The specimen and its record belong together

**Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History — Paleobiology Collections Search**  
https://collections.nmnh.si.edu/search/paleo/

Museum catalog records connect specimens with identification, collection, location, morphology, geochronology, and stratigraphy. The archive scene pairs a stored object with a record and a stable internal study identifier. Its labels describe an interpretive artwork; they do not imitate the accession record of an actual museum specimen.

## Interpretation policy

- The narrative is a material and scientific interpretation, not a taxonomic reconstruction of a particular fossil.
- No generated specimen is assigned a measured geological age, real excavation locality, or verified species identification.
- Section indices refer to the supplied synthetic data. They do not claim scanner resolution or physical dimensions.
- Missing material remains visibly partial. The work does not use invented scientific confidence percentages.
- Museum and geological references support terminology and reasoning. Their inclusion does not imply institutional affiliation or endorsement.

## How the references enter the finished exhibit

| Scene system | Research translated into interaction |
| --- | --- |
| Strata and Burial | Irregular material beds and a covering front express deposition and relative sequence, without inventing equal time intervals. |
| Preservation | Four native controls distinguish pore infill, replacement, mold and cast. A shared silhouette provides continuity while the material record changes. |
| Exposure | The reveal slider and optional dust brush expose small areas; remaining matrix supports the trace. The gesture is an interpretation of careful preparation, not a practical preparation procedure. |
| Amber | Transmitted light and a minute indeterminate inclusion express enclosure; no identifiable insect anatomy or viable original tissue is claimed. |
| Scan and Reconstruct | A generated photograph gives way to a contour and then synthetic sections. All 72 sections share a fixed coordinate frame; a partial isosurface comes from the same field. These are data-consistent artistic representations, not measurements from the depicted object. |
| Archive | A drawer, paper supports, native catalog text and a downloadable JSON record give the study a readable identity. That identity remains explicitly internal to the artwork. |

The numerical source is documented in [volume-metadata.json](../../public/fossil/data/volume-metadata.json) and [data/README.md](../../public/fossil/data/README.md). The photographic source and the synthetic field are separate authored representations; matching their narrative role does not imply that one was scientifically acquired from the other.
