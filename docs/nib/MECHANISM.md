# Mechanism — Qualitative Model and Limits

NIB depicts a selected conventional slit-nib fountain pen, an aqueous dye ink and ivory writing paper. Source identifiers refer to [RESEARCH.md](./RESEARCH.md). Geometry, scale and timing are authored for observation, not measured from a named product.

## Reservoir and replacement air

The reservoir contains liquid and an air volume. As ink is consumed, replacement air is admitted through the feed arrangement. “Balance” means sustained regulated supply; it does not mean identical pressure everywhere or a continuously open connection to the atmosphere. The experimental cartridge/feed study describes a changing holding pressure and intermittent bubble admission [R1–R4].

The return-air animation should therefore remain sparse. Opposite travel directions communicate exchange, but their visible speeds and event counts are not a volumetric calculation. No displayed pressure is a measured value.

## Feed and slit

The principal ink route is longitudinal and narrow. Transverse comb grooves provide temporary storage for excess ink; the narrative must not imply that normal delivery traverses every fin in sequence. The nib sits closely against the feed so ink can cross into the slit [R1, R2, R7].

A narrowing slit is a chosen geometry, not a claim about every nib. Stronger capillary attraction does not automatically mean faster flow: hydraulic resistance and wetting also matter. The visible nib hole is not the complete pressure-regulation system. Its mechanical role and air connection depend on the construction [R5, R6, R12].

## Meniscus and contact

The tip’s liquid surface is greatly magnified. The held bead is an expressive view of a small attached meniscus, not an assertion that a correctly functioning pen normally accumulates a large hanging drop. It should not grow indefinitely during a paused scene.

The rounded tipping area approaches stationary paper. Contact creates a liquid connection through which the paper draws ink. The tip need not puncture the surface or press hard to initiate delivery. Gold nib material and hard wear-resistant tipping are separate parts of the visual anatomy [R2, R5, R7].

## Paper, stroke and drying

Paper uptake follows spaces within a fibrous structure. Fine branching may become visible under magnification, but the resulting mark remains a controlled stroke. This artwork selects dissolved dye ink, so it does not portray a suspension of coarse pigment particles [R1, R9].

Absorption and evaporation overlap. The wet highlight fades while colored material remains; the entire ink does not vanish. A matte final trace is the selected ink/paper finish, not a universal property of all fountain-pen inks. Dramatic oxidative blackening is omitted because it belongs to particular formulations [R8–R10].

## Interpretation and reversible viewing

Scroll controls authored scene state. Reversing restores earlier visual states, rather than claiming real drying or paper absorption reverses spontaneously. Living holds may alter reflections and small interface motion, but must not imply unlimited ink production. Magnification, bubble timing, line width and transition duration are compositional choices. The experience explains relationships without asserting exact pressures, dimensions, flow rates or drying times.
