# Research — NIB: Ink Under Pressure

Accessed: **2026-10-07**.

## Research position

NIB follows a conventional fountain pen as a coupled liquid-and-air system. Manufacturer explanations consistently connect capillary delivery, replacement air and paper uptake. Experimental work adds an essential qualification: the reservoir does not remain at one perfectly equalized pressure. A changing pressure difference and a capillary valve regulate air admission and continued ink supply [R1–R4].

The visual interpretation therefore follows one narrow ink route, accompanied by a much quieter return-air route. It keeps the metal, feed, liquid and paper physically distinct. This is a qualitative narrative, rather than a reconstruction of a particular commercial pen.

## Primary references

**R1 — PILOT, “Why does fountain-pen ink flow smoothly?”**  
https://www.pilot.co.jp/media/knowledge/008.html  
Explains capillary channels, comb grooves that temporarily retain excess ink, replacement air through the feed, and paper fibers drawing ink from the tip. This is the main accessible source for the full narrative.

**R2 — Platinum, “Fountain-pen mechanism.”**  
https://www.platinum-pen.co.jp/about-fountain-pen/6/  
Confirms paper-driven uptake, air replacement and buffering. Also describes formed nib alloys, attached hard tipping material, molded feeds and surface treatment that helps ink wet the feed.

**R3 — Japan Writing Instruments Manufacturers Association, “Fountain-pen characteristics and mechanism.”**  
https://www.jwima.org/knowledge/types/fountain-pen/structure/  
Corroborates the roles of the feed’s ink passage, air passage and overflow grooves. Its explanation connects environmental pressure changes with excess ink supply.

**R4 — Nikolov, Murad, Wasan and Wu, “How the capillarity and ink-air flow govern the performance of a fountain pen,” Journal of Colloid and Interface Science 578 (2020), 660–667.**  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcis.2020.04.123  
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021979720305877  
Abstract index: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32559481/  
Accessible abstract: https://agris.fao.org/search/en/providers/122535/records/65dfb8a34c5aef494fe4aae9  
The reported mechanism involves reservoir holding pressure, a meniscus at a capillary valve and air bubbles that relieve the holding pressure. The publisher’s full page returned HTTP 403 during research; the full abstract was read through AGRIS. No claim here depends on inspecting inaccessible experimental figures.

**R5 — Sailor, “Fountain-pen structure.”**  
https://sailor.co.jp/topics/fountain-pen-structure/  
Distinguishes nib alloy, wear-resistant tipping, slit, heart hole and feed. Describes the hole’s influence on elasticity and the feed’s liquid/air exchange and buffering functions.

**R6 — Sailor, “How a fountain pen is made.”**  
https://sailor.co.jp/topics/process/  
Documents rolling, punching, forming, tipping attachment, slit cutting, polishing and nib/feed adjustment. It also describes the hole as an air inlet in its construction.

**R7 — PILOT Library, “Fountain-pen structure.”**  
https://www.pilot.co.jp/library/001/structure.php  
Describes hard tipping material, close nib/feed contact, ink delivery and excess-ink retention. Body examples include resin, metal, ebonite and lacquer finishes.

**R8 — Kim et al., “Nanofountain Pen for Writing Hybrid Plasmonic Architectures,” Small Structures (2024).**  
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sstr.202300260  
Its introductory fountain-pen analogy distinguishes overlapping absorption and evaporation on paper. Its nanoparticle-printing dimensions and deposition model are not applied to ordinary dye ink in NIB.

**R9 — Sailor, “The difference between dye and pigment inks.”**  
https://sailor.co.jp/faq/fountain-pen-ink-type/  
Distinguishes dissolved dye from insoluble pigment and explains that written-line behavior also depends on paper.

**R10 — Platinum, ink information and FAQ.**  
https://www.platinum-pen.co.jp/about-fountain-pen/388/  
https://www.platinum-pen.co.jp/contact/faq/  
Differentiates ordinary dye inks, carbon pigment inks and traditional iron-containing formulations. Oxidative darkening is not treated as a universal drying mechanism.

**R11 — Aurora, technical FAQ.**  
https://aurorapen.it/faq/  
Identifies ebonite as its feed material and provides nib and reservoir anatomy. Material claims are understood as descriptions of Aurora’s products.

## Supplementary firsthand engineering analysis

**R12 — Amadeus W., “Nippy Nibs,” Fountain Pen Design.**  
https://fountainpendesign.wordpress.com/fountain-pen-nib-design-function/  
An engineer’s firsthand construction analysis, not a peer-reviewed paper. It explains nib/feed crossover and why a visible hole’s air function depends on the feed layout. It is used with manufacturer descriptions, not as evidence that every pen has the same arrangement.

## Visual research and asset boundary

The reference pages informed nib curvature, polished tipping, engraved recesses, repeated feed pockets, liquid interfaces and fibrous paper. Visual emphasis belongs to thin reflections, narrow wet edges and strong changes of scale. The final trace uses a restrained dark dye appearance on warm ivory paper.

These sources are **reference-only**. No copyrighted source photography, diagrams or article figures were downloaded or incorporated by this research pass. A bibliography does not grant image-reuse rights.
