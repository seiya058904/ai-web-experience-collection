# URUSHI — Research & Material Direction

Research checked: 7 October 2026.

## Scope

This work follows one imagined wooden vessel through a compressed, artistically
directed account of lacquer finishing. It is **not a universal urushi process
manual**, a conservation guide, or a numerically calibrated materials simulation.
Methods differ between regions, workshops, objects, and finishes.

The mirror surface is **roiro-inspired**. The earlier **Polish** scene presents a
prepared surface and a first moment of clarity; it does not declare the object
formally finished before its subsequent decoration, covering, and reveal.

AI-generated studies and self-authored models, textures, shaders, and renders are
visual interpretations. They are **not historical photographs, workshop records,
or scientific evidence**. The institutional images below informed research; their
availability online does not grant permission to redistribute them.

## Authored visual interpretation

The following are design decisions, not measured physical specifications.

- **One persistent form.** Camera crops may change; the vessel should remain
  recognizable through its silhouette and continuous surface coordinates.
- **Reflection carries progress.** A long, soft reflected light is weak on wood,
  interrupted during abrasion, and more coherent after polishing. Surface
  transformation has priority over camera spectacle.
- **Black remains the subject.** Warm brown-black, neutral dark reflections,
  subtle texture, and partially lost edges give the image depth. Black layers
  should not resemble a stack of clear glass shells.
- **Humidity is quiet.** Enclosed space, restrained atmospheric density, and a
  held composition suggest controlled curing. Steam, sunlight, and running
  droplets are not used as a literal explanation of hardening.
- **Abrasion stays on the surface.** Fine directional marks and softened
  reflections convey wet charcoal work. Sparks and large dust clouds would
  distort the scale and character of the process.
- **Vermilion is a material turn.** It shares the vessel's established reflection
  language and remains non-emissive.
- **Gold arrives late.** A limited deposition field catches powder in a defined
  design. Settled particles remain attached. Small angle-dependent highlights
  replace glowing particle trails.
- **Reveal has a boundary.** The covered design reappears through an advancing
  abrasion/polish mask. A uniform gold opacity fade is insufficient.
- **Nashiji is optional and specific.** If used, it is a local study of metallic
  flecks beneath a warm translucent layer, not a synonym for all maki-e.
- **Layer sections are conceptual.** Any enlarged cross-section or accelerated
  sequence communicates accumulation; it does not assert a real layer count,
  thickness, duration, or universally correct curing condition.

## Copy guardrails

Use language such as “a prepared surface,” “roiro-inspired finish,” “a compressed
study,” and “moisture, air, and time.” Do not claim that every object requires the
same number of layers, that every layer cures in a fixed number of hours, or that
one humidity value is a universal rule. Do not describe early polishing as the
completion of all work if a later scene shows togidashi decoration.

Useful original scene lines:

- **Before depth, grain.**
- **The unseen holds the finish.**
- **Stillness does the work.**
- **Lose the shine. Refine the surface.**
- **Depth is accumulated time.**
- **Light finds continuity.**
- **Gold, held by lacquer.**
- **Covered. Cured. Polished into view.**
