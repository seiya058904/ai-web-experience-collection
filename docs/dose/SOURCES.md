# DOSE — Scientific sources

Research checked on 12 October 2026. This register records the scientific background used for the artwork, the location where each source is relevant, and what was actually accessible during research.

DOSE depicts a hypothetical tablet. It does not claim conformity with a pharmacopoeia, validated manufacturing process, regulatory approval, or measured product performance. The sources below inform qualitative explanations; they are not a manufacturing specification.

## Sources used

### 1. FDA — Active and inactive ingredients

**Source:** [Inactive Ingredients in Approved Drug Products Search: Frequently Asked Questions](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/inactive-ingredients-approved-drug-products-search-frequently-asked-questions), questions 1, 2 and 7.

**Used in:** Material chapter; About panel; [process explanation](PROCESS.md).

**Relevant point:** The API has the intended drug activity. Other formulation ingredients can have useful functions; the word “inactive” should not be read as a statement that an ingredient has no physical influence. The artwork assigns no identity or therapeutic effect to its hypothetical active ingredient.

**Access:** The FDA page was opened and its relevant text read.

### 2. FDA / ICH Q8(R2) — Pharmaceutical development

**Source:** [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/q8r2-pharmaceutical-development) and [full guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/71535/download), November 2009; parent guidance, section 2.1.2, “Excipients.”

**Used in:** Material chapter and its material-role legend; About panel.

**Relevant point:** Excipient function and physical characteristics matter to formulation and manufacture. The legend is an illustrative grouping of roles, not a complete ingredient inventory or a recipe. “Matrix” describes the surrounding formulation material here; it does not designate a controlled-release system.

**Access:** Both FDA landing page and full guidance PDF were opened. No numerical limits, design-space example, or manufacturing condition was transferred to DOSE.

### 3. Ishikawa et al. — Direct compression and granulation

**Source:** [Manufacturability and Properties of Granules and Tablets Using the Eco-Friendly Granulation Method Green Fluidized Bed Granulation Compared to Direct Compression](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/cpb/69/5/69_c20-00970/_html/-char/en). *Chemical and Pharmaceutical Bulletin*, 69(5), 447–455, 2021. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.c20-00970).

**Used in:** Selected manufacturing branch; Distribution and Press chapters; About panel.

**Relevant point:** Direct compression is a distinct powder-preparation, blending and tableting route. The paper compares it with a granulation route, supporting the decision to keep those branches separate. Material suitability is formulation-dependent.

**Access:** Full text, including the direct-compression workflow, was opened and read. The real formulation, experimental settings, measurements and paper figures are not reproduced.

### 4. FDA — Mixing and segregation background

**Source:** [Oral Solid Dosage Forms Pre/Post Approval Issues (1/94)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/inspection-guides/oral-solid-dosage-forms-prepost-approval-issues-194), January 1994. Relevant sections cover drug-substance characterization, mixers, and tablet equipment.

**Used in:** Distribution chapter and the distinction between visual arrangement and measured uniformity.

**Relevant point:** Differences in particle properties can affect segregation during mixing and subsequent handling. A visually even powder animation cannot establish blend quality.

**Access:** Relevant FDA page text was opened and read. This is a **historical inspection reference** used for stable qualitative background. Its historical numerical criteria and procedural guidance are not treated as current requirements.

### 5. Tay et al. — Particle surface properties and compaction

**Source:** [Effects of Particle Surface Roughness on In-Die Flow and Tableting Behavior of Lactose](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/effects-of-particle-surface-roughness-on-in-die-flow-and-tabletin/). *Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences*, 108(9), 3011–3019, 2019. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xphs.2019.04.028).

**Used in:** Press chapter; qualitative particle-rearrangement concept.

**Relevant point:** Particle rearrangement and surface properties contribute to compaction behavior. DOSE simplifies these effects into an observable change from powder bed to coherent form.

**Access:** The author-institution record and its complete abstract were opened. The journal full text was not inspected. The study's material preparation is not inserted into the chosen manufacturing route.

### 6. FDA / ICH Q6A — Quality attributes

**Source:** [Q6A Specifications: Test Procedures and Acceptance Criteria for New Drug Substances and New Drug Products: Chemical Substances](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/q6a-specifications-test-procedures-and-acceptance-criteria-new-drug-substances-and-new-drug-products), especially section 3.3.2.1.

**Used in:** Evidence chapter; About panel; definitions of distinct quality questions.

**Relevant point:** Appearance, dosage-unit uniformity, disintegration and dissolution address different attributes. A cutaway rendering is not an analytical test or a demonstration that finished units meet a specification.

**Access:** The full guidance text on the FDA page was opened. [USP's dosage-unit FAQ](https://www.usp.org/frequently-asked-questions/uniformity-dosage-units) supplied supplementary indexed text, but its direct page returned an access error. A full current USP chapter review is not claimed.

### 7. Ferdoush et al. — Water uptake and micro-CT

**Source:** [Fast time-resolved micro-CT imaging of pharmaceutical tablets: Insights into water uptake and disintegration](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37918497/), 2023. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpharm.2023.123565). [Open-repository record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10786181/).

**Used in:** Surface, Evidence and Release chapters; visual research context.

**Relevant point:** Research has used micro-CT to investigate liquid entry through tablet pore networks, swelling and disintegration. This supports the choice of subject, not the accuracy of DOSE's invented internal geometry.

**Access:** Indexed abstract and bibliographic information were available. Direct repository access encountered a browser check, and the publisher page returned an access error. The original scan dataset and full image series were not inspected or reused.

### 8. USP — Disintegration

**Source:** [Official historical harmonization PDF for chapter <701>](https://www.usp.org/sites/default/files/usp/document/harmonization/gen-chapter/april-2019-m99460.pdf), April 2019, and [USP bibliographic preview](https://doi.usp.org/USPNF/USPNF_M99460_04_01.html).

**Used in:** Release chapter and About panel.

**Relevant point:** Disintegration does not establish complete dissolution of the dosage form or its active material. The distinction is the basis of the artwork's explanation of breakup and solution as different processes.

**Access:** Relevant historical PDF text was accessible through the official source's search index; direct PDF retrieval failed. The bibliographic preview opened, but it does not provide the full current chapter. No current test protocol or acceptance limit is claimed.

### 9. FDA — Dissolution testing

**Source:** [Dissolution Testing of Immediate Release Solid Oral Dosage Forms](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/dissolution-testing-immediate-release-solid-oral-dosage-forms) and [guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/70936/download), particularly the background section.

**Used in:** Evidence and Release chapters; About panel.

**Relevant point:** Dissolution testing addresses drug substance entering solution. Absorption and clinical response involve additional factors, so an invented liquid-contact sequence cannot predict them.

**Access:** The FDA guidance PDF was opened and relevant text read. No curve, release-time claim, test condition, or clinical prediction is reproduced.

## Reference and asset boundaries

Scientific attribution does not provide permission to reuse a source's photographs, scans, figures, trademarks or datasets. The application uses original procedural geometry and material textures. Its conceptual imagery, colors and particle distributions are not extracted from the studies above. Fonts and software have separate licenses recorded with the project.

The ISPE training link in the supplied brief was an initial research lead. Its page could not be read because of access restrictions, so it is not relied on as inspected evidence in this register.
