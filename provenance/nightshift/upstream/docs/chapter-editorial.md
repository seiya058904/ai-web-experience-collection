# NIGHTSHIFT: chapter editorial ledger

Editorial edition: 2026-11-09. All text in this file is English. **Meridian City, its locations, its workers, and every time below are fictional.** The times are suggested narrative markers, not real schedules or claims about when particular work must occur. An on-screen time should be accompanied by an available, readable fiction statement such as `An imagined night in Meridian City`.

This ledger specifies content and interaction outcomes. It does not claim that an image, motion, or browser test has already been completed. The exact required primary copy is preserved. Supplemental copy is an optional writing resource and should appear only where it serves the chapter's two beats.

## CH-05 · A Clean Start

Suggested fictional time: **02:34**. Role: make one maintenance action and its local result visible.

**Morning has to be made.**

A careful sweep changes the ground that thousands of footsteps will soon cross.

**Beat A — Before:** Leaves, grit and small debris interrupt a particular curb. Keep a recognizable seam, drain or fixed edge in both states.

**Beat B — After:** The debris is cleared from that same place and the curb edge becomes legible. Keep the camera, weather, buildings and lighting consistent.

Supplemental copy: `The reflection was here all along. The cleared edge makes it easier to see.`

Primary interaction uses `Before`, `After`, `Compare`, and `Reset comparison`. The accessible range label is `Compare the street before and after sweeping`. A value description can be `50% after sweeping`; reset restores 50%. Both endpoints must be reachable through on-screen buttons and the keyboard. Descriptive alternatives: `Leaves and grit collect beside the wet curb.` and `The same curb after leaves and grit have been cleared.`

Connection: two converging street lines align with rails only after an opaque structural edge separates the spaces.

## About this experience

> Meridian City is an original fictional composite. Its streets, people and times are invented. This is a portrait of quiet preparation, told through imagined rooms, surfaces and gestures. The atlas and diagrams are editorial illustrations. They are not live services or operational instructions.

> The amber line is a way of looking from one scene to the next. It does not join these workers into a single system. Each place keeps its own purpose, and each person remains more than the task we glimpse.

## Proofreading and inclusion checks

- Keep all meaningful text selectable in HTML or accessible SVG. Do not bake any of this text into generated images.
- Preserve the exact primary sentences above; headings should not become generic slogans.
- Keep supplemental passages short enough for stable reading holds. Do not place old and new captions in the same reading zone during handoff.
- Do not quote fictional workers as though these were interviews. No invented statistics, institutions, brands, certifications, medical assertions, or technical readouts are needed.
- The same two beats and interaction outcomes must remain accessible in portrait and reduced-motion views.
- The care vignette contains no patients. Transit maintenance contains no approaching operational train. The final city must visibly retain the opening window.
