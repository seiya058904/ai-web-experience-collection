The geometry of a fold.

One line.

A fold is a rotation about a straight line. Nothing stretches and nothing tears. Whatever was on the reverse of the paper now faces you.

Four lines meet.

Where creases meet at a point, they move together. This one closes completely only if alternating angles add up to 180°. That rule is Kawasaki's theorem. Move one crease and try it.

Notes and sources

The folding here is exact, not animated by hand. Each facet stays a rigid plane in every frame, and the tests check that for every pattern. The paper has zero thickness and collisions between layers are not modelled, so real paper stops a little sooner.

The geometry

  • Kawasaki's theorem. A vertex can fold flat only if alternating angles sum to 180°.
  • Maekawa's theorem. At a flat-folded vertex, mountains and valleys differ by two.
  • Schenk and Guest, 2013, PNAS. The Miura cell described by facet sides, a sector angle and one fold angle.

The history

  • Heidelberg Laureate Forum, Origami mathematics. The Miura fold on the Space Flyer Unit, launched in March 1995. Accounts of the flight and of when Miura first proposed the pattern vary, and we did not find a primary record from JAXA.

Reading the printed pattern

Blue dash-dot lines are mountains and fold away from you. Red dashed lines are valleys and fold toward you. Thin paper and a ruler help. Pre-crease every line before you try to collapse the sheet.
