FUSION / FIELD NOTES

Inspired by physics.
Made of light.

This is an original artistic interpretation of a tokamak, built entirely from geometry, shaders, particles and sound. It is not a reconstruction of a specific facility or a numerical fusion simulation.

THE MACHINE

Vertical toroidal field coils, horizontal poloidal rings and a central solenoid surround a toroidal vacuum vessel. A sectional opening reveals the plasma within.

THE FIELD

Toroidal and poloidal magnetic components combine into twisted paths. Charged particles follow these fields; uncharged fusion neutrons can leave the plasma.

THE NUMBERS

Temperature, field excitation and boundary deviation are illustrative. Colors, particle sizes, timescales and wave amplitudes are artistic. “Ignition” names the narrative beginning, not technical fusion ignition.

THE FUTURE

The final energy pathway is a concept. No net electricity, power-plant performance or real experimental discharge is claimed.

The story of FUSION

A procedural journey through eight stages of magnetic-confinement fusion. The visuals and readings are illustrative.

  1. Ignition

    A first current starts the narrative. The word “ignition” here means the beginning of the experience.

  2. The machine

    A vacuum chamber, toroidal and poloidal field coils, central solenoid and divertor surround the plasma.

  3. Confinement

    Toroidal and poloidal magnetic fields form twisted paths that help confine charged plasma particles away from the walls.

  4. Plasma

    Heating ionizes the gas and energizes its particles. The experience illustrates a temperature approaching 150 million degrees Celsius.

  5. Instability

    Disturbances can disrupt magnetic confinement and increase energy loss.

  6. Control

    Measurements inform adjustments to magnetic fields and heating to manage the plasma boundary.

  7. Star

    Deuterium and tritium can fuse into helium-4 and a neutron, releasing 17.6 MeV per reaction. Charged helium can heat the plasma. Neutrons carry energy outward.

  8. Future

    Future fusion plants aim to turn captured heat into useful electricity. This artwork does not claim net power or plant performance.
