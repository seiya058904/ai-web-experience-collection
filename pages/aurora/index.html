AURORA

A storm you can see.

It starts as wind.

The Sun sheds a thin stream of charged particles. Earth's magnetic field holds it off, tens of thousands of kilometres out. Turn the wind's own field south and the two join. Energy gets in.

It lands in a ring.

Stored energy falls along field lines toward both poles and arrives in two ovals. They stay fixed toward the Sun while Earth turns beneath them. The dashed line is you.

Oval edge at midnight

Colour is altitude.

Each colour is one gas at one height. Oxygen near 100 km glows green within a second. Far higher, thin oxygen glows red, slowly. Ionised nitrogen adds violet at the edges of strong storms.

How this was made

Nothing here is a photograph or live data. The curtain, the lake and the magnetosphere are computed in your browser. Earth's surface is the NASA Blue Marble.

Published relationships used

  • Shue et al., 1998, J. Geophys. Res. The boundary's distance and flare, from wind pressure and field direction. Coefficients as reproduced in Annales Geophysicae, 2025.
  • Newell et al., 2007, J. Geophys. Res. The rate that magnetic flux opens, from wind speed, field strength and direction.
  • SpaceWeatherLive, the Kp index. Kp against the geomagnetic latitude of the oval's edge.
  • NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center, Aurora. Where the light forms and how storms move it.
  • Whiter et al., 2023, Annales Geophysicae. Measured heights of green 557.7 nm and blue 427.8 nm aurora.
  • AuroraWatch UK, Lancaster University. Green and red emission lines and their lifetimes.

Where this is illustration

  • Turning wind coupling into a Kp value is our own compression, not a forecast model.
  • The oval's width and day-side shape, the curtain's folds and rays, and the flow around Earth are drawn to explain, not measured.
  • Latitude here means geomagnetic latitude, which differs from the latitude on a map. Clouds, moonlight and city light are not modelled.
  • For a real night out, check a live service such as NOAA's aurora forecast.
