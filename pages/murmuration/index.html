FIELD NOTES

A few rules.
An unexpected whole.

A local-rules model inspired by flocking research. Each bird makes its next move from a small nearby world: keep space, match heading, stay together.

There is no designated leader in this model. A soft environmental boundary keeps the flock within view. There are no target outlines or scripted formations.

The observation is a reversible recording of an actual simulation. The live field runs forward in its own time. Bird scale, color and camera movement are artistic choices.

Research & resonance

  1. Craig Reynolds · Boids 1987 ↗

    Separation, alignment and cohesion as local steering behaviors.

  2. Ballerini et al. · A nearby world 2008 ↗

    The studied starling flocks interacted with roughly six or seven neighbors. Our bounded spatial search approximates up to seven nearby agents; it is not an exact biological model.

  3. Attanasi et al. · A traveling turn 2014 ↗

    Observed turning information spread through starling flocks. This artwork does not reproduce the paper's specific dynamics.

  4. RANDOM INTERNATIONAL · Swarm Study / I ↗

    The visitor's presence as part of a responsive collective.

  5. DRIFT · Flylight ↗

    An artwork exploring the individual and the group.
